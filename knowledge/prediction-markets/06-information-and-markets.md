# 06 — Information and Markets

## Information flow

Prediction markets move when information changes beliefs.

Information sources include:
- official releases (economic data, court decisions, election updates)
- expert analysis
- local or niche observations
- interpretation of ambiguous news

## News impact

Not all news has equal effect.

Large repricing usually requires news that is:
- credible
- relevant to resolution criteria
- unexpected relative to prior beliefs

Markets often react first to the *implication* of news, not just the headline.

## Information aggregation

Core idea: many participants each know something different. Trading combines these fragments into one market price.

Why this is powerful:
- incentives encourage quick and honest belief updates
- disagreement is productive; it creates trades and price discovery
- aggregated prices can outperform single experts in many domains

## Efficient-markets intuition

An efficient prediction market quickly incorporates publicly available information.

In practice, efficiency is conditional:
- better with higher liquidity and broader participation
- weaker in thin, hard-to-verify, or highly emotional markets

## Delayed reactions

Markets can underreact when:
- information is complex
- few specialists are active
- execution frictions are high
- participation is temporarily low

Researchers often study post-news drift to measure this.

## Behavioral effects

Even incentive-driven markets can reflect human biases:
- overconfidence
- herding
- motivated reasoning (especially in political events)
- attention shocks (traders focus on salient stories)

These effects can create temporary divergence between price and eventual outcome frequency.

## Why this chapter matters for research

This is where economics and market data meet:
- price paths encode belief updates
- order flow shows who acted on information
- liquidity determines how cleanly information becomes price

A strong research workflow treats each price move as a joint product of **information + incentives + microstructure constraints**.
