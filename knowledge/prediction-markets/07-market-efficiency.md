# 07 — Market Efficiency

## Efficient market concept

A prediction market is efficient if prices reflect available information quickly and without systematic bias.

In that case, predictable excess errors are hard to find after accounting for costs.

## Why markets may still be wrong

Markets are estimates under uncertainty, so errors are normal.

Common reasons:
- genuine uncertainty (future is not yet determined)
- noisy or incomplete information
- low liquidity and high transaction costs
- ambiguous resolution risk

## Biases and behavioral frictions

Even with money on the line, biases can persist:
- **Overconfidence**: placing too much weight on favored scenarios
- **Recency bias**: overweighting latest news
- **Confirmation bias**: seeking evidence that supports prior view

## Political distortions

Political markets can be distorted by:
- expressive trading (trading identity, not information)
- partisan narratives
- coordinated attempts to signal confidence

Distortion can be temporary if informed traders and arbitrageurs have enough capacity.

## Herd behavior

Traders may follow price moves rather than independent analysis.

Effects:
- short-term momentum
- crowded positioning
- delayed reversal when deeper information emerges

## Practical efficiency mindset for engineers

Ask:
1. Is this signal supported by liquidity and depth?
2. Did the move follow high-quality information?
3. Is the apparent edge still present after spread/fees/slippage?
4. Does this pattern persist out of sample?

## Key point

Efficiency is not all-or-nothing. Prediction markets are often *locally efficient but occasionally fragile*, which is exactly why they are scientifically interesting.
