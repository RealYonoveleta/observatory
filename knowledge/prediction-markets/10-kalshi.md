# 10 — Kalshi Overview

## Platform role

Kalshi is a U.S. event-contract exchange operating under a regulated framework, with contracts typically settling in USD based on objective event outcomes.

## Event contracts

Kalshi contracts are structured around clear yes/no event questions with defined payout rules and resolution sources.

Research implications:
- cleaner legal/operational definitions
- strong emphasis on formal contract terms
- easier comparisons for certain regulated event classes

## Regulatory model

Kalshi emphasizes CFTC oversight and exchange-style controls (market integrity, surveillance, customer protections).

Why this matters:
- contract design and listing constraints differ from crypto-native platforms
- participant access and product scope are shaped by regulation
- data and workflow may be more standardized in some dimensions

## Market structure

Like other event markets, Kalshi uses:
- event definitions
- tradable outcome contracts
- continuous repricing through trading activity
- settlement after objective resolution

## Similarities vs Polymarket

- Both translate uncertain events into tradable probability-like prices.
- Both rely on explicit resolution criteria.
- Both can be analyzed through market microstructure and forecast evaluation lenses.

## Differences vs Polymarket

- **Regulatory context**: Kalshi is CFTC-regulated; Polymarket has a different regulatory/operational model.
- **Market design constraints**: listing, participation, and contract governance differ.
- **Infrastructure conventions**: quote conventions, API workflows, and data surfaces may differ.

## Research framing

Use Kalshi when you want to study prediction markets in a regulated event-contract setting, and compare findings against Polymarket to isolate effects of:
- regulation
- participant mix
- liquidity profile
- contract design choices

Primary starting points:
- `https://kalshi.com/learn`
- `https://kalshi.com/market-integrity/regulation`
