# 01 — Introduction

## What is a prediction market?

A prediction market is a market where people trade contracts tied to future events. The price of each contract is interpreted as a probability estimate.

Example: if "YES" on "Will event X happen?" trades at 0.70 (or 70 cents), the market is saying the event is roughly 70% likely.

## Historical background

Modern prediction markets draw from:
- earlier political betting markets
- futures market design
- mechanism-design research in economics
- electronic market infrastructure

Academic and applied work (including Iowa Electronic Markets and later research) showed markets can often produce competitive forecasts relative to polls and expert judgment.

## Why prediction markets emerged

They emerged because organizations needed better ways to aggregate dispersed information:
- experts know different pieces of reality
- incentives are often weak in ordinary forecasting discussions
- binary outcomes allow clean evaluation after the fact

Markets solve this by attaching cost/reward to expressed beliefs.

## Famous examples

- **Iowa Electronic Markets**: early academic benchmark for election forecasting.
- **Intrade (historical)**: popularized event-contract trading to wider audiences.
- **Polymarket**: high-activity crypto-native event market with open data interfaces.
- **Kalshi**: CFTC-regulated U.S. event contract exchange.

## Why this matters for researchers

Prediction markets are not only trading venues; they are data-generating systems for studying:
- belief updates
- information diffusion
- market efficiency
- calibration of probabilistic forecasts
